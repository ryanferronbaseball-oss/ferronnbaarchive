THE METHODOLOGY

Star power, not game quality.

Only players who appeared

Each individual is counted once per attended game, even if they earned several awards. Nonparticipating backups never count.

Career Legacy lens

Counts players with verified lifetime honors—including later Hall of Fame inductions, MVPs and professional achievements. Its Star Power Index reflects how much collective career prestige was on the field or court.

Prime at Game lens

Uses the season of the game and dated individual award evidence. Same-season MVP, All-League and All-Star honors carry full weight; honors one season away count at 70% and those two seasons away count at 32%. Honors more than two seasons away do not establish that a player was in his prime at the game.

The Prime Era Index aggregates each player's proximity to his strongest documented award seasons; it is a retrospective proxy, not a precise measure of physical prime or game performance. “Before peak” and “After peak” mean relative to the dated awards currently recorded. Draft status, future stardom, team championships and Hall of Fame induction alone never establish peak-season status. College football and some development-league appearances have insufficient year-specific award coverage and are explicitly marked “Not rated.”

Coverage is limited to sports with linked player appearances and verified achievement files. An athlete absent from this list is not necessarily unaccomplished; some historical honors or game rosters are still being researched. Prime scores should be compared most carefully within a sport, since award coverage varies. The page refreshes as the underlying data improves.
